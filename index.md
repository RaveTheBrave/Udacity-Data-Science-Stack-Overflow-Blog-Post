
Artificial intelligence has quickly become part of everyday software development. Developers now use AI tools to write code, troubleshoot problems, learn new technologies, and complete repetitive tasks. But is AI being adopted equally across the profession? And does using AI relate to salary or how developers feel about the future of their careers?

Using responses from the Stack Overflow Developer Survey, I explored three questions:

1. How does AI adoption vary across different career stages?
2. Is AI usage associated with differences in developer salary?
3. How does AI usage relate to developers’ views about AI threatening their jobs?



AI use was common across every career stage, but adoption declined as professional experience increased.

About **83.1% of early-career developers** reported using AI tools, compared with **81.9% of mid-career developers**, **79.2% of experienced developers**, and **71.4% of highly experienced developers**.

This suggests that developers earlier in their careers are somewhat more likely to incorporate AI into their work.



Surprisingly, AI users did not earn more overall.

The median annual salary for AI users was approximately **$74,772**, compared with about **$80,871 for non-AI users**.

However, the difference was small when compared with the wide range of developer salaries in the survey. This suggests that AI usage alone does not explain how much a developer earns. Experience, job role, location, and other factors are likely much more important.



Using AI did not necessarily make developers feel safer about AI’s impact on employment.

Approximately **15.2% of AI users** said AI threatens their current job, compared with **11.2% of non-AI users**. Still, most respondents in both groups said AI was **not** a threat to their current job.



AI has become widely adopted among developers, but its impact is not the same for everyone. Younger-career developers appear more likely to use it, AI usage alone does not lead to higher salaries, and even developers who already use AI can remain concerned about its effect on their careers.

The results suggest that AI is becoming another important tool in software development—but its relationship with careers is more complicated than simply using AI or avoiding it.
